If you manage blood sugar over the long term, choosing the right app is almost as important as choosing the right glucose meter. **mySugr** has been a well-known name in the space for years, while **SugarLite** is gaining attention with lower pricing and stronger AI features.

Why is SugarLite worth recommending in the second half of 2026? The answer is in the comparison below.

## 1. Pricing: The most obvious advantage

| Plan | mySugr Pro | SugarLite |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | $2.99/mo | **$0.99/mo** ✅ 67% less |
| Yearly | $27.99/yr | **$9.99/yr** ✅ 64% less |
| Lifetime | ❌ Not available | **$29.99 once** ✅ |

The key difference: mySugr is subscription-only. Over five years, that adds up to roughly **$140**. SugarLite offers a **$29.99 lifetime purchase**—about the cost of **13 months** of mySugr Pro.

For anyone who needs long-term, often lifetime, glucose management, that's a very practical saving.

## 2. Feature comparison: Beyond logging, toward understanding

### AI food logging: from "photo memo" to "snap and done"

| | mySugr | SugarLite |
|---|---|---|
| Meal photos | Pro feature; stores photos only | AI recognizes food and nutrition from a photo |
| Logging method | Manual input | Snap a photo; the entry is created automatically |
| Retry on failure | — | Retries allowed without extra credit charges |

In mySugr, a meal photo is basically a visual reminder—you still type in what you ate. SugarLite's **AI food logging** turns one photo into a complete log, including food type and nutrition data. That saves time and removes the friction of manual entry.

### Personalized Glycemic Index (PGI): a SugarLite exclusive

- **mySugr**: Shows glucose curves and estimated HbA1c—telling you *whether* your glucose is high
- **SugarLite**: Analyzes repeated meal-glucose pairs to show you *which foods* raise your blood sugar and by how much, in a dedicated PGI leaderboard

This is a difference in product philosophy. mySugr answers "what happened"; SugarLite answers "what caused it."

Because each person's gut microbiome, insulin sensitivity, and metabolism differ, generic GI tables can only go so far. A personalized GI table is far more useful. Read more in our guide: [What Is PGI (Personalized Glycemic Index)?](/en/blog/what-is-pgi)

### Tracking coverage: oral meds get their own entry point

| Record type | mySugr | SugarLite |
|---|---|---|
| Blood glucose | ✅ | ✅ |
| Meals | ✅ (manual) | ✅ (AI photo + manual) |
| Exercise | ✅ | ✅ |
| Insulin | ✅ (calculator in Pro) | ✅ |
| Oral medication | ⚠️ Limited | ✅ First-class, dedicated entry point |

SugarLite treats oral medications as a first-class record type, not an afterthought in a notes field.

### Health platforms and hardware: the basics, plus widgets

| | mySugr | SugarLite |
|---|---|---|

SugarLite also supports an **iOS home screen widget**, so you can glance at key data without opening the app.

### Daily experience: small details add up

| | mySugr | SugarLite |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose units | ✅ | ✅ Free switch between mmol/L and mg/dL |
| Dark mode | ❌ | ✅ System / light / dark modes |
| Measurement reminders | Pro only | ✅ Local reminders + 3-day recall prompts |
| Reports | CSV free; PDF/Excel require Pro | Built-in trends and stats in Insights tab |

Guest mode and the 3-day recall reminder are especially helpful for new users: try the full app before signing up, and get nudged to fill in missed readings so your data stays complete.

## 3. Where mySugr still wins

For fairness, here are areas where mySugr remains stronger:

- **Bolus Calculator**: A Pro feature in mySugr. SugarLite currently logs insulin but does not suggest doses.
- **Device ecosystem**: mySugr supports more devices, such as Novo Pen 6 and Libre.
- **Brand backing**: Part of Roche. Accu-Chek users on certain meters can get Pro for free.
- **Maturity**: Launched in 2012 with a large user base and a recognizable monster mascot.

If you rely heavily on the Roche ecosystem or need an integrated bolus calculator, mySugr is still a safe choice.

## 4. The bottom line
